## [Einstein's relativity rules chemical bonds in heavy elements](https://www.brown.edu/news/2026-07-09/chemical-bonds-relativity) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers at Brown University used photoelectron spectroscopy to provide direct experimental evidence that relativistic effects govern chemical bonding in heavy elements like bismuth, confirming theoretical predictions from the 1970s. This study fundamentally challenges the textbook model of chemical bonds taught in high school for heavy elements, and provides the first clear spectroscopic signature of relativistic bonding, which could reshape our understanding of heavy-element chemistry and materials science. The team studied bonds between carbon and bismuth, and identified sigma and pi bonds whose properties are strongly influenced by spin-orbit coupling — a relativistic effect where an electron's spin and orbital motion are no longer independent.

hackernews · hhs · Jul 10, 22:30 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48866134)

**Background**: In heavy elements, electrons near the nucleus travel at a significant fraction of the speed of light, making relativistic effects (described by Einstein's special relativity and Dirac's equation) crucial. These effects alter electron orbitals, leading to phenomena such as the yellow color of gold and the liquidity of mercury at room temperature. Relativistic quantum chemistry has long predicted such influences, but direct experimental evidence for chemical bonds was lacking until now.
