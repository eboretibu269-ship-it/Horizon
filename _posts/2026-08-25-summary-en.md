## [FDA authorizes first wearable dual glucose-ketone monitor](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-authorizes-first-wearable-device-continuously-monitors-both-ketone-levels-and-blood-sugar) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The FDA has authorized Abbott's Libre Duo 10 Day, the first wearable device that continuously measures both ketone levels and blood sugar. The device uses a single sensor worn on the body and streams data to a smartphone app. This approval marks a milestone in metabolic health monitoring, giving people with diabetes a convenient way to watch two critical biomarkers simultaneously. It could improve early detection of diabetic ketoacidosis and open the door to more integrated wearable biosensors. The sensor measures glucose and ketone concentrations in interstitial fluid every minute over a 10-day wear period, transmitting readings wirelessly to a compatible smartphone. Although marketed as a wearable, it is not truly noninvasive: like a CGM, it relies on a small sensor filament inserted just under the skin.

hackernews · sunnynagra · Aug 25, 19:07 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49439017)

**Background**: Glucose is the body's main sugar fuel, while ketones are produced when the body breaks down fat for energy, often during fasting, low-carb diets, or insulin deficiency. Continuous glucose monitors (CGMs) are widely used by diabetics to track blood sugar, but ketone testing has traditionally required blood or urine strips. Abbott and other companies have been working on combining both measurements into a single wearable biosensor, similar in size to existing CGM sensors.

## [OpenAI's Custom 'Jalapeño' Chip Outperforms Nvidia Blackwell in Tests](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/openai-jalapeno-better-than-nvidia) ⭐️ 8.0/10

OpenAI has released first benchmark results for its custom ASIC inference chip, Jalapeño, claiming it outperforms Nvidia's Blackwell-based GB300 on efficiency and latency. In tests on models such as GPT-OSS 120B and DeepSeek R1 670B, the chip delivered 1.5–1.9x better throughput per watt and 1.7–3.6x lower end-to-end latency. This signals that large AI labs are moving seriously toward custom inference silicon to cut costs and gain leverage over Nvidia, potentially reshaping the AI hardware economics. If the claims hold, it could pressure Nvidia's dominant data center GPU position and accelerate falling token prices. The chip was co-developed with Broadcom, has a rated power of 700W but sustained power draw under 550W in testing, and was benchmarked against Nvidia's GB300 rather than the newer Vera Rubin. It is designed only for inference, not training; OpenAI plans to deploy it in its own data centers by the end of the year, with a second generation in development and a third in design.

**Background**: An ASIC (application-specific integrated circuit) is a chip customized for a particular use rather than general-purpose processing, offering higher performance per watt for tasks like AI inference. Inference is the stage where a trained machine-learning model makes predictions on new data, and it is increasingly the dominant cost for AI services. Custom inference chips are emerging as a way for large model providers to reduce dependence on general-purpose GPUs.

Firefox 157 will enable JPEG XL (JXL) image support by default across all platforms, so users get the next-generation image format without needing any opt-in. This makes Firefox a major browser to embrace JXL as a standard feature. Default JPEG XL support gives web developers and users a modern image format with significantly better compression, lossless quality, and HDR capabilities, improving web performance and image fidelity. It also adds momentum to browser ecosystem adoption, especially as Chromium appears to be pursuing a similar path via the Rust-based jxl-rs library. Discussion in the dev-platform thread notes that both Firefox and Chromium efforts are built around the Rust-based jxl-rs library, while Apple has already shipped the C++ libjxl implementation. Commenters also raised questions about benchmark comparisons between the two libraries and about support for legacy Windows 7/8 users on Firefox 115.

hackernews · yboris · Aug 25, 17:55 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49437946)

**Background**: JPEG XL is a free and open image format defined by ISO/IEC 18181, developed by the JPEG committee together with Google and Cloudinary. It supports both lossy and lossless compression and offers significantly better image quality and compression ratios than legacy JPEG, while being designed for efficient software decoding even on mobile devices. Despite these advantages, browser adoption has been uneven, making default support in Firefox a significant milestone.
